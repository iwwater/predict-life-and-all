# Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos · Selected Excerpts (Western Astrology)

> Claudius Ptolemy's *Tetrabiblos* (c. 150 CE) is the foundational text of Western astrology.
> Excerpts below are from the F.E. Robbins translation (Loeb Classical Library, 1940), public domain.

## Book I · Chapter 2 · On the Power of the Planets

The active power of the sun's essential nature is found to be heating and, to a certain degree, drying. The moon has for its most characteristic power that of moistening, because of its proximity to the earth.

Saturn's effect is chiefly to cool and to dry, but probably also to impair. Jupiter's is to heat and to moisten, and to do so beneficially. Mars's is to dry and to burn. Venus's is to moisten moderately and to warm. Mercury's is to be drying and absorptive in a moderate degree.

*Source: Tetrabiblos I.2, Ptolemy*

## Book I · Chapter 11 · On the Signs

Of the twelve signs, some are masculine and some feminine. The masculine signs are Aries, Gemini, Leo, Libra, Sagittarius, and Aquarius. The feminine signs are Taurus, Cancer, Virgo, Scorpio, Capricorn, and Pisces.

The signs are also classified by triplicity according to the four elements: Aries, Leo, and Sagittarius are fiery; Taurus, Virgo, and Capricorn are earthy; Gemini, Libra, and Aquarius are airy; Cancer, Scorpio, and Pisces are watery.

*Source: Tetrabiblos I.11, Ptolemy*

## Book I · Chapter 13 · On the Aspects

The aspects are distances between planets measured along the zodiac. The opposition (180°) and the trine (120°) are the most powerful. The square (90°) is less harmonious. The sextile (60°) is weaker but still cooperative. The conjunction (0°) carries the character of both planets merged.

*Source: Tetrabiblos I.13, Ptolemy*

## Book III · Chapter 13 · On the Quality of Action

We must not suppose that everything that happens to men is caused by heavenly influence as by a divine ordinance. Rather, the celestial bodies produce certain natural changes, while each person's individual character, nurture, and circumstances contribute to the outcome.

*Source: Tetrabiblos III.13, Ptolemy*
